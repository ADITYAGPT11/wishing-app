# Win India's occasions by owning the reminder loop

The market gap is not another invitation maker. **No product found, global or Indian, links the whole loop of remembering an occasion, making a wish or invite, sending it, collecting RSVPs and reminding guests.** None adds Indian calendar intelligence either: tithi (lunar) birthdays, festival dates, or multi-function weddings where each guest sees only their own functions. Basic RSVP is now free from the platforms: WhatsApp Events (2024), Apple Invites (2025) and Snapchat Plans (September 2026). Generic festive greetings are free through Meta AI inside WhatsApp. A newcomer cannot win on either feature alone. It can win as a **family occasion calendar** that turns each date into a personal wish or an ad-free RSVP page, shared over WhatsApp with no signup for guests. Demand is large but unmeasured. India has 8–10 million weddings a year (about $130B), more than 500M WhatsApp users and a 34–35M-strong diaspora, yet no credible market size exists for e-invites. A solo developer can build and run the MVP for ₹0 on Cloudflare Workers, Supabase Free, self-sent Web Push, Resend or Brevo email and `.ics` calendar files, as long as they avoid five traps:

- Supabase pauses free projects after one week of inactivity.
- Vercel's Hobby plan is non-commercial.
- Panchang code built on Swiss Ephemeris carries the AGPL licence.
- Phone OTP and automated WhatsApp messages are paid.
- Most free asset licences forbid using the assets to build a template library or a competing service.

Revenue should come from paid wedding and multi-function packs, paid WhatsApp automation, NRI pricing and festive gifting affiliates, never from ads on guest pages. One evidence caveat applies throughout: **no Reddit threads or first-hand user quotes were retrieved.** Most pricing and pain-point evidence comes from competitor blogs, vendors and app trackers.

## Platforms made RSVP free, but no one owns the occasion loop

The global market splits into four camps:

1. **RSVP-first event apps** with no or low fees and a curated look: Partiful, Apple Invites, Luma, Snapchat Plans and WhatsApp Events.
2. **Designed-invitation platforms** that charge per event, per guest or by subscription: Evite, Paperless Post and Greetings Island.
3. **General design tools** with strong editors and little or no native RSVP: Canva and Adobe Express.
4. **E-card and group-card sites** with no event loop: Jacquie Lawson, American Greetings, Hallmark, Kudoboard and GroupGreeting.

The decisive change from 2024 to 2026 is that platforms gave RSVP away. **WhatsApp Events** now works in groups and 1:1 chats. It has yes/no/maybe replies, plus-ones, and an automatic reminder for guests marked "going" ([WhatsApp blog](https://blog.whatsapp.com/new-feature-roundup-updates-to-group-chats-events-calls-channels-and-more)). People without WhatsApp can RSVP through a link without installing anything ([WhatsApp blog](https://blog.whatsapp.com/whatsapp-events-brings-people-together-beyond-the-group-chat)). **Apple Invites** lets anyone RSVP free on any device. Creating an event, though, needs iCloud+ (from $0.99/month) and an iPhone ([MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2026/11/11/apple-invites-gets-two-new-features)). Apple has updated it almost monthly through 2026 with co-hosts, polls and AI backgrounds ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/23/apple-invites-app-gets-co-hosting/); [AppleWorld.Today](https://appleworld.today/2026/10/apple-updates-its-invites-app-to-take-advantage-of-new-ios-27-features/)). **Snapchat Plans** launched on 10 September 2026, aimed squarely at Partiful ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/10/snapchat-takes-aim-at-partiful-with-new-event-planning-features/)). Partiful itself shows what a free, link-based core can do. Sensor Tower estimated **about 500K average MAU in Q1 2025, up about 400% year on year**, with 9 in 10 users in the US. Its first paid product is ticketing ([NBC/CNBC](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/meet-partiful-the-gen-z-party-planning-staple-thats-taking-on-apple/6231135/)).

The paid incumbents charge US prices. Evite shows third-party ads to guests on free invites and charges about **$17.99–$99.99 per event** for Premium ([usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/evite-pricing/)). Paperless Post uses per-recipient coins, and Paperless Pro costs **$250/year for 250 guests**, in USD only ([Paperless Post pricing](https://paperlesspost.com/pricing)). Both carry Diwali and Indian-wedding templates: Haldi, Mehndi and Sangeet invitations, plus Anita Dongre designs on Paperless Post ([Paperless Post](https://www.paperlesspost.com/cards/group/indian-wedding); [Evite](https://www.evite.com/invites/wedding/wedding-weekend/indian-wedding)). For Indian occasions these are template categories, not workflows. Canva is the only Western player found with INR pricing (**₹4,000/year Pro**), and it has no native RSVP ([Canva India pricing](https://www.canva.com/mr_in/pricing/); [Jotform](https://www.jotform.com/blog/canva-rsvp/)).

India repeats the split in four silos, and none connects them.

- **Weddingkart** is the closest threat. It is WhatsApp-native, sends invites in 11 Indian scripts, runs a voice-AI RSVP agent in 14 languages, and keeps one master list across mehendi, sangeet, wedding and reception. It is wedding-only and costs **₹4,999–₹8,499 per wedding plus GST** ([StartupTalky](https://startuptalky.com/weddingkart-success-story/)).
- **Template apps** cover design only and carry no RSVP: Crafty Art (500K+ installs on its flagship app, [AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/dev/Crafty+Art/)), Celebrare (₹500–₹3,000 bundles, [Weddingkart blog](https://www.weddingkart.co/blog/best-ai-wedding-invitation-generators)) and WedMeGood's invite maker.
- **Tithi-birthday reminders** exist only in small single-purpose apps: Nutan Vedic Panchang, Tithi Tracker, Sampangi and KUDUMI ([App Store: Nutan Vedic Panchang](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6757436721); [Sampangi](https://sampangi.pages.dev/)). Panchang giants Drik Panchang and Kalnirnay have no wishes or invites.
- **Name-on-greeting apps** (photo frames and similar) are ad-funded and do nothing beyond the image ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/app.diwali.photoeditor.photoframe/keywords)).
- **Freelancers and studios** charge **₹499 to ₹25,000+** for static or video invites that go out as WhatsApp JPGs, PDFs or MP4s with no RSVP ([LBB Delhi](https://lbb.in/delhi/websites-wedding-invitations-online/); [Happiffie](https://www.happiffie.com/hyderabad/share-unforgettable-moments-video-invitations)).

## A $130B wedding economy with no measured e-invite market

The demand pool is very large, but the specific market is unmeasured. Jefferies (June 2024) puts India at **8–10 million weddings a year and about USD 130B in spending**, second only to food and grocery, with an average of about ₹12 lakh per wedding ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2024/06/25/weddings-now-a-dollar130-billion-industry-expenses-only-seen-going-up-disproportionately-in-most-cases.html)). The trade body CAIT projected **about 46 lakh weddings and ₹6.5 lakh crore of business** in the 1 November–14 December 2025 season alone ([CAIT](https://cait.in/wedding-season-2025-to-generate-₹6-5-lakh-crore-business-from-46-lakh-weddings-across-india-cait-delhi-alone-to-witness-₹1-8-lakh-crore-trade-from-4-8-lakh-weddings-indian/)). Other estimates range from $50B to $104B, so the headline is an order of magnitude, not a precise figure. **No credible size exists for Indian or global digital invitations, or for e-cards.** The global greeting-card market is about $20B in 2025 by paywalled analyst estimates ([Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/industry-reports/global-greeting-card-market.md); [TechSci](https://www.techsciresearch.com/report/greeting-cards-market/24830.html)). "Event management software" estimates of $7–16B mostly measure corporate conference tooling ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/event-management-software-market); [Grand View](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/event-management-software-market-report)). Do not pitch either as the TAM.

A bottom-up sketch is more honest. If 1% of 8–10M weddings bought a ₹500–₹2,000 digital pack, revenue would be about **₹4–20 crore a year** (roughly $0.5–2.4M). That is an illustrative calculation, not a sourced figure. Birthdays, griha pravesh, naming ceremonies, pujas and festivals add volume at lower willingness to pay, but they bring year-round use, which wedding tools lack.

Four trends shape the product.

- **WhatsApp is the default channel.** Meta reported more than **500M Indian WhatsApp users** in December 2024. Third-party estimates are about 532–536M ([Backlinko compiling Meta/DataReportal](https://backlinko.com/whatsapp-users)). The festival peaks are huge, but the official figures are old. Indians sent 14B WhatsApp messages on New Year's Eve 2016 ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/technology/science/14-bn-whatsapp-msgs-sent-on-new-year-in-india-1483690475.html)). No newer Diwali figures were found.
- **AI greetings went mainstream in Diwali 2025.** Viral uses included Gemini "Nano Banana" portraits and Meta AI generating wish images and stickers inside WhatsApp ([Storyboard18](https://storyboard18.com/digital/diwali-2025-nano-banana-ai-prompts-turn-diwali-selfies-into-dazzling-couple-portraits-82718.htm); [Gizbot](https://www.gizbot.com/how-to/diwali-2025-how-to-create-and-share-animated-stickers-ai-generated-wishes-and-gifs-on-whatsapp-119893.html)). Brands also pay to sit inside festive greetings, as Coca-Cola and Google did with "Festicons" ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/mktg/coca-cola-and-google-launch-ai-festicons-for-diwali-celebrations-10560249)).
- **The diaspora is a sizeable audience across time zones.** The government counted about **34.36M Indians abroad** as of January 2025 ([MEA Lok Sabha answer](https://www.mea.gov.in/Images/CPV/LS196_A.pdf)). Secondary sources split this into about 15.85M NRIs and 19.57M PIOs ([VisaVerge](https://www.visaverge.com/news/half-of-overseas-indians-live-in-just-10-countries-worldwide/)).
- **Free link-based tools can grow fast.** Partiful's growth on a free, no-install core supports this.

The implication is that generic AI images and generic RSVP are commodities. The defensible layer is personal context: who the person is, their relationship to you, their tithi date, the user's language, and timing.

The resulting architecture:

- **Front end:** a PWA built with SvelteKit or Next.js, served from Cloudflare.
- **Back end:** Supabase for Postgres, Google sign-in and email magic links for hosts, and Edge Functions.
- **Guest RSVP:** no login. Each guest gets a link with a random, unguessable token per invite, so invitees arriving from WhatsApp never create an account.
- **Reminders:** one pg_cron job every 5–15 minutes selects due reminders and sends Web Push and email through an Edge Function.
- **Calendar:** "add to calendar" as `.ics` downloads, Google and Outlook deep links, and a per-user `webcal://` feed of yearly occasions. This uses no Google Calendar API, so there is no OAuth verification. The approach is standard, but the specific URL formats and the `ics` npm package were not checked against docs this session.

Two items are not strictly free. The first is a **custom domain**. It is needed for SPF/DKIM email deliverability, and the notes state from unverified prior knowledge that Resend needs a verified domain to email arbitrary recipients. Start on a free `*.pages.dev` or `*.workers.dev` subdomain and buy a domain before email reminders go live; domain prices were not verified. The second is **paid upgrades once you have traction**, chiefly to remove Supabase's pause. Analytics free tiers (PostHog, Sentry, Umami) were not verified.

## Compute festivals yourself, because the best panchang code is AGPL

The licence trap here is serious. **Swiss Ephemeris**, the engine under nearly every serious panchang library, is dual-licensed: AGPL, or a **700 CHF** Professional licence. Under AGPL the whole project must be AGPL, and the choice must be made before "any public service using the developed software is activated" ([Astrodienst](https://www.astro.com/swisseph/swephinfo_e.htm); [price page](https://www.astro.com/swisseph/swephprice_e.htm)). The most complete libraries are separately AGPL on top of that. **drik-panchanga** is AGPL-3.0 on pyswisseph ([GitHub](https://github.com/webresh/drik-panchanga)). **PyJHora** (AGPL-3.0-or-later, v5.0.0, released 8 November 2026) supports both amanta and purnimanta months and adhika-month flags, and ships a multilingual festival CSV ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/PyJHora/)). Buying the Swiss Ephemeris licence therefore does **not** cure PyJHora's or drik-panchanga's copyleft. Running either on a server behind a closed-source app would oblige you to publish the whole app.

The clean route is **astronomy-engine** (MIT; JavaScript, Python, C, C# and Kotlin). It is accurate to **±1 arcminute** and validated against NOVAS and JPL Horizons. It finds any Sun–Moon elongation and computes sunrise ([GitHub](https://github.com/cosinekitty/astronomy)). Those are the only primitives needed:

- **Tithi** = floor(((Moon longitude − Sun longitude) mod 360) / 12) + 1.
- **Tithi of the day** is the tithi in force at local sunrise.
- **Nakshatra** uses sidereal (Lahiri) lunar longitude.
- **Amanta month name** comes from the Sun's sidereal sign at the preceding new moon.

One arcminute is roughly two minutes of time, which is fine except when a tithi ends within minutes of sunrise. Permissive alternatives exist but need checking. **jyotisha** is MIT, but its dependencies were not confirmed, and it may pull in pyswisseph ([GitHub](https://github.com/jyotisham/jyotisha)). **mhah-panchang** is MPL-2.0, a file-level copyleft, with zero runtime dependencies; its accuracy and ayanamsa were unverified ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/mhah-panchang/latest)). A second workaround is to run an AGPL tool offline, generate a static date table for 2025–2035, and ship only the data. The argument is that computed astronomical facts are not copyrightable, but this is a common position, not legal advice.

The proposed **tithi-birthday algorithm** is an engineering inference; no source fetched documents a canonical one.

1. Record the birth location. Compute the tithi at sunrise on the birth date, the amanta month, and whether that month was adhika (intercalary).
2. Each year, find the same-named regular (nija) month.
3. Within that month, pick the civil day whose sunrise tithi matches, using the user's current city.

The edge cases need explicit rules and a visible note to the user:

- **Kshaya tithi** (a tithi that prevails at no sunrise).
- **Vriddhi tithi** (one that spans two sunrises; conventionally use the first day).
- **Birth in an adhika month.**
- **Births before sunrise**, which belong to the previous day's tithi.
- **Purnimanta relabelling** for North Indian users.
- **Diaspora date shifts** between birthplace and current city.
- **Solar-calendar communities** (Tamil, Malayali, Bengali, Odia), which often use janma-nakshatra birthdays instead.

Validate a test set against a trusted panchang such as Drik Panchang before launch.

For **festival dates**, no free, licence-clean API covers Indian regional festivals well. Calendarific's free tier gives **500 calls/month with attribution required**; Starter costs $12/month ([Calendarific](https://calendarific.com/pricing)). Prokerala's free tier gives **5,000 credits/month at 5 requests/minute**, with commercial terms unstated, and its paid plans start at ₹999/month ([Prokerala](https://api.prokerala.com/pricing)). Nager.Date returned a 404 for India ([nagerholidays.com](https://nagerholidays.com/Country/IN)). The practical answer is a **curated yearly festival JSON of a few hundred rows**:

- Tithi festivals computed from rules, for example Ganesh Chaturthi on Bhadrapada shukla 4.
- Solar festivals from the Sun's sidereal ingress (Makar Sankranti, Vishu, Baisakhi), with Onam tied to Thiruvonam nakshatra.
- Islamic dates shown as "expected" with an admin override, because Indian moon sighting can differ by a day from computed calendars.
- Each year cross-checked against a published panchang.

Browsers' `Intl` handles the Saka and Islamic calendars but **has no Hindu lunisolar calendar** ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Reference/Global_Objects/Intl/Locale/getCalendars)).

## Free assets stay free only until they become your template library

A curated-asset product runs into the exact clauses free libraries use to protect themselves: no packs, no core app content, and no "similar or competing service."

| Source | Commercial use / credit | Trap for a template product |
|---|---|---|
| unDraw | Commercial OK, no credit ([licence](https://undraw.co/license)) | Bans distributing "in packs", replicating a competing service, using illustrations as the core of an app, and AI training. Fit for UI only |
| Freepik (now Magnific) | Free use **requires attribution** ([terms](https://www.magnific.com/legal/terms-of-use)) | No use as the main element of resold apps, and no inclusion in a "library" |
| Unsplash | Commercial OK, no credit ([licence](https://unsplash.com/license)) | No compiling images into a similar or competing service; no selling without significant modification |
| Pexels | Commercial OK, no credit; **"templates you sell" explicitly allowed** ([licence](https://www.pexels.com/license/)) | No selling unaltered copies; no showing identifiable people in a bad light |
| Pixabay (images and music) | Commercial OK, no credit ([terms](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/)) | No "standalone" redistribution; no competing service; no ML scraping |
| LottieFiles (free animations) | Commercial OK, no credit ([Lottie Simple License](https://lottiefiles.com/page/license)) | Modified files stay under the same licence; no compiling files into a competing service |
| Google Fonts / OFL 1.1 | Bundle and self-host freely ([OFL text](https://openfontlicense.org/open-font-license-official-text/)) | Cannot sell the font on its own. Rendered cards are free of obligations |
| OpenMoji | CC BY-SA 4.0 with a credit line ([FAQ](https://openmoji.org/faq)) | ShareAlike: recoloured emoji shipped in cards must be CC BY-SA. Prefer Twemoji (CC BY 4.0, aggregator-sourced) |
| YouTube Audio Library | YouTube-only; Google gives no guidance for use off the platform ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882)) | Do not use in a web app |

The safe pattern is to **compose assets into finished templates** (crop, overlay, typography, colour grade) and serve only the composed result, never a browsable or downloadable gallery of raw photos or animations. Indian motifs such as diya, rangoli, kalash, toran and mandala should be self-drawn, commissioned or AI-generated and then hand-edited.

AI-generated art is cheap to make but **not protectable in the US**. The Copyright Office (January 2025) says prompts alone do not make you the author ([IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/01/29/part-two-copyright-office-ai-report-says-creative-prompting-doesnt-constitute-authorship/)). India's position on AI outputs is unsettled. DPIIT's December 2025 working paper dealt only with training ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/dpiit-releases-working-paper-on-copyright-and-ai-proposes-hybrid-licensing-model-85551.htm)). The upshot is that competitors can copy AI-made templates freely.

Avoid Bollywood or film songs entirely; label and collecting-society rights make them an infringement magnet. Avoid deity artwork you didn't create or license, and avoid brand logos. Licences for several popular sources were **not verified** this session: Open Peeps, Humaaans, Blush, Storyset, SVGRepo, the Lucide/Phosphor/Tabler/Heroicons icon sets, Incompetech, Free Music Archive, Uppbeat and IconScout. Read each LICENSE before shipping.

## Keep the core free and charge for weddings, WhatsApp automation and NRIs

Comparable products all keep the basic invite free and charge for premium layers, which validates the model. Partiful's core is free and it earns from ticketing, reportedly "around 10% + $2" per ticket according to a competitor ([Luma](https://help.luma.com/p/luma-vs-partiful)). Luma charges 5% on paid tickets on its free plan ([Luma pricing](https://luma.com/pricing)). Paperless Post's first sends can be free ([usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/paperless-post-pricing/)). US price points are far out of reach in India. Realistic anchors are much lower:

- **Floor:** free (WhatsApp Events).
- **Canva Pro:** ₹4,000–4,500/year; Pro Lite reportedly ₹199/month ([Techmitra](https://techmitra.in/what-is-canva-features-pricing-and-ai-explained/)).
- **Drik Panchang ad removal:** ₹19/month or ₹199/year, a low anchor for calendar features ([Drik Panchang T&C](https://www.drikpanchang.com/contactus/privacy-policy/app/dp-apps-terms-and-conditions.html)).
- **Weddingkart:** ₹4,999–₹8,499 per wedding.
- **Video invites:** ₹1,499 to ₹25,000+ ([WedMeGood](https://www.wedmegood.com/blog/10-gorgeous-video-wedding-invites-that-cost-under-5k); [VenueLook](https://www.venuelook.com/costs/invitation-prices-in-india)).

Against an average wedding spend of about ₹12 lakh, a ₹999–₹2,999 pack is 0.1–0.25% of the budget. Willingness to pay will turn on quality and social signalling, not affordability.

The revenue lines that keep the core free, in order of fit:

1. **A paid wedding / multi-function pack (suggested ₹499–₹1,999).** It unlocks sub-events, per-function guest tags and RSVPs, more guests, premium themes and an export. This undercuts freelancers and Weddingkart while matching the strongest evidenced pain.
2. **Paid WhatsApp automation.** Reminder and RSVP-confirmation messages through the Cloud API cost you about ₹0.115 each (utility category), so a ₹99–₹299 add-on per event has healthy margin. Meta verification is a prerequisite.
3. **NRI/global pricing in USD**, anchored well below Evite's $17.99+ per event.
4. **Festive gifting affiliates.** IGP pays about 7.2% CPS and FNP about 7%, so roughly ₹35–₹210 per gift on ₹500–₹3,000 orders. Peaks are seasonal: Diwali, Rakhi, and NRIs sending gifts home ([GrabCash](https://grabcash.in/blog/?p=2149); [Commission Factory](https://www.commissionfactory.com/advertiser-directory/fnp-affiliate-program/66373)).
5. **Sponsored festive templates**, following the Festicons precedent.
6. **A later B2B "team birthdays" tier.** Indian recognition suites charge about $3–5 per employee per month ([Empuls](https://intercom.help/empuls/en/articles/11142554-empuls-pricing)).

Avoid ads on guest-facing pages; that is the pain point you are exploiting. Avoid **pooled group-gift money**. RBI's 2025 Payment Aggregator Master Directions require authorisation and escrow for anyone holding collected funds ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/financial-services/1681402/rbi-rewrites-the-payment-aggregator-rulebook)). If group gifting comes later, use direct UPI intents to the organiser's own VPA with pledge tracking only, or a licensed PA partner, and get legal confirmation either way. The price points in this list are recommendations derived from the anchors, not tested prices.

## Position as the family occasion calendar, and ship the loop first

**Recommended positioning:** "Never miss a birthday, tithi or festival again. Turn any occasion into a beautiful wish or an RSVP invite in a minute, shared on WhatsApp, with no app, no signup and no ads for your guests. Made for Indian families everywhere."

This positioning concedes nothing to the platforms. Against WhatsApp Events it adds design, memory and the Indian calendar. Against Meta AI it adds personal timing and context. Against Weddingkart it is year-round with a free core. Against Paperless Post and Evite it offers INR pricing, WhatsApp-first sharing and Indian occasions as workflows rather than template categories. The recurring calendar is the retention engine, because wedding tools see a user once per wedding. It is also the cheapest growth loop: every wish and invite is a branded link opened inside WhatsApp.

| Build first (MVP, about 6–10 weeks solo, a rough estimate) | Defer until validated |
|---|---|
| Host sign-in with Google and email magic link; guests never sign in (token links) | Phone OTP (paid per SMS; DLT registration needed) |
| Personal occasion calendar: Gregorian birthdays and anniversaries plus a curated festival list (Hindu, Sikh, Jain, Christian, Islamic marked "expected") for the next 2 years | Full regional festival coverage; per-community calendars |
| **Tithi birthdays, labelled beta**: amanta computation on astronomy-engine, purnimanta display, current-city sunrise, explicit notes on edge cases | Nakshatra birthdays, Shraddha/Barsi, ekadashi-style recurring tithi reminders |
| Wish creator: about 10 occasions × 3–5 curated themes; editable text, a fixed colour palette, one photo, one effect (confetti or diyas); Hindi + English text | Freeform editor, user-uploaded music, video export, AI image generation, more Indic scripts |
| Single-event invites with RSVP (yes/no/maybe, headcount, note), host dashboard, edit and broadcast updates | **Multi-function events with per-function guest tags** (the first paid pack) |
| Share via `wa.me` and Web Share; pre-rendered OG images (1200×630, under 300 KB) | Automated WhatsApp Cloud API messages (paid add-on) |
| `.ics` / Google / Outlook add-to-calendar, plus a personal webcal feed | Two-way Google Calendar sync (OAuth verification) |
| Reminders via Web Push (with an iOS "Add to Home Screen" coach mark) and email, driven by pg_cron | Native apps; SMS reminders |
| Sensible limits: up to about 150 guests per event, 1 photo, curated assets only, a fair-use cap on email reminders | Group gifting/UPI, gifting affiliates, B2B team walls, a creator template marketplace |

The limits protect the free tiers as well as the design quality. Fixed palettes and curated assets also keep you inside the licence clauses above. The calendar's first real test is the next festival window, Makar Sankranti and Pongal in mid-January, followed by the spring wedding season.

## Eight risks and the open questions that decide them

The product risks:

- **Platform risk.** WhatsApp could add themes and reminders to Events at any time. Snap and Apple are shipping monthly.
- **Correctness and sensitivity risk.** A wrong tithi date or festival on a religious occasion costs more trust than a missed feature. Edge cases must be visible and overridable, and user-generated cards with deity imagery need reporting and moderation. The notes flag Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita religious-offence provisions as a risk, but did not verify them against the statute.
- **Free-tier risk.**
  - Supabase pausing breaks guest links in quiet weeks.
  - Free tiers change without notice. Netlify moved to credits, and Firebase moved Storage behind Blaze.
  - Resend's 100 emails/day caps email-heavy reminders.
  - Vercel Hobby forbids commercial use, so do not launch paid features there.
- **iOS reach.** Web Push requires a home-screen install, so iPhone guests and hosts need email or WhatsApp fallbacks.
- **Licensing and copying.** The AGPL trap and the template-library clauses are covered above. AI-made templates cannot be protected from copying.

The open questions need answers before scaling, and most can be settled cheaply:

- Will hosts enter relatives' birthdays, and birth place and time for tithi? Storing third parties' personal dates also raises a privacy-policy question the notes did not research.
- Do older relatives actually tap RSVP links, or is in-chat WhatsApp RSVP essential, which is the paid API?
- Is ₹499–₹1,999 the right wedding-pack price?
- Is Supabase Cron enabled on the Free plan, and does R2 need a card?
- Does a lawyer accept shipping data pre-computed with AGPL tools?
- Do Calendarific's and Prokerala's free tiers allow commercial use?
- What do the unverified Indian competitors (Seventy Two Invites, Celebr8, Nuvo and others) actually offer?

The evidence gaps are material and should be closed by fieldwork, not more desk research:

- No Reddit or first-hand quotes, and no Play Store low-star review sample.
- No measured share of digital versus printed invites in India.
- No WhatsApp festival-volume figures after 2019.
- Partiful's ticketing launch date conflicts across sources (2025 vs June 2026).
- Competitor prices for Evite, Punchbowl, Kudoboard and American Greetings come from aggregators and competitors, not the vendors' own pages.
- Market-size figures are paywalled headlines with conflicting scopes.

Twenty interviews with Indian and NRI families who hosted an event this year, plus a manual Reddit and Play Store sweep, would settle more than another round of searching.

## Conclusion

The research changes what kind of product this is. It reads at first as a design product, a better Canva for Diwali cards. The evidence says it is a **memory-and-timing product** with design attached. Platform giants commoditized the two features that look most valuable, RSVP and AI greetings, in the same two years, and they left behind what the platforms structurally avoid: an Indian family's private calendar of lunar birthdays, festivals and multi-function events, and the nudge that turns each date into a message. That layer also suits a zero-budget solo build. It runs on cheap cron jobs and small date tables rather than heavy media, and it compounds: every remembered date brings the user back every year, while a wedding tool sees them once.

The decisive engineering choice comes before any code: **build tithi and festival logic on MIT-licensed astronomy-engine, not on Swiss Ephemeris-based libraries.** Choosing wrong silently commits the entire app to AGPL or a licence fee. The decisive business choice is to make the first paid product the multi-function event pack, because that is where pain, willingness to pay and Indian specificity overlap most clearly. Neither choice has been validated with real users yet. That is the next job, and it costs nothing but conversations.
